CAMERA — this is a game level background seen from directly above.
- Camera directly overhead at 90 degrees: a top-down game-map view, like an architectural floor
  plan painted with real materials, or a room photographed from straight above through a glass
  ceiling.
- ORTHOGRAPHIC: no perspective at all. Parallel lines stay parallel; the floorboards must be the
  SAME WIDTH at the top edge of the image as at the bottom edge; no vanishing point, no
  converging lines, no foreshortening.
- EVERY VISIBLE SURFACE IS HORIZONTAL. No vertical surface may be visible anywhere: no front face
  of a bookshelf (no rows of spines seen face-on), no side panel of the counter, no shaft of a
  baluster, no side of a bench, no rung of a ladder seen from the side. You see only tops and
  footprints. If you can see the height of any object, the view is wrong.
- No horizon line.

SPACE BUDGET — THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT REQUIREMENT. An earlier attempt failed because the
furniture ate the playable area; only a fifth of the image was walkable.
- OPEN, WALKABLE FLOOR: at least 80% of the image area. This is where the player moves around and
  fights. It must be one continuous open floor, not broken into small pockets.
- Everything that blocks movement (bookshelves, counter, furniture, balustrade, void) together:
  at most 20% of the image.
- The bookshelves: ONE narrow strip along the TOP border only, about 5% of the image, hugging the
  edge, with their TOPS as thin flat wooden caps. Nothing along the bottom border, and nothing
  reaching into the middle of the room.
- The balustrade and the void: ONE narrow strip along the LEFT border only, about 8% of the image
  in total.
- The landmark counter: SMALL and slim, lying along the lower-right at an angle, about 5% of the
  image, leaving the entire centre open.
- Furniture (lamp, teapot, benches, book stacks, ladders): small individual objects, widely
  spaced, a couple of percent each at most.
- A player must be able to walk between any two objects: leave wide open lanes between all of
  them. No large dark masses anywhere.
- The RIGHT edge and the BOTTOM edge must be plain open floor continuing off the image — a
  neighbouring image continues the same floor there.

EXPOSURE — this background is played on, so it must be clearly readable. Hard requirements:
- Mid-tones dominate. Overall brightness around 100/255: a dim but CLEARLY LIT room, not a dark
  room, not a mood painting.
- At least half of the image sits in the mid-tone range (roughly #5a6a88 to #d8dce8).
- No more than about 15% of the image may be darker than #3a4258, and none of it may be pure
  black.
- NO CAST SHADOWS AT ALL: do not paint any directional shadow on the floor from the balustrade,
  the counter, the shelves, the ladders or the benches — no long dark diagonal bands, no dark
  side of the counter. A soft warm CIRCULAR pool of lamplight is allowed; directional shadow
  shapes are not.
- EVEN LIGHTING — a hard requirement, and the thing an earlier attempt got wrong: the floor is
  lit UNIFORMLY across the entire image. Brightness must NOT be one bright pool with dark
  surroundings. Sample any two patches of open floor anywhere in the image and they should read
  within about 2x of each other; the darkest part of the floor should be no less than half the
  brightest part. Aim for the image's MEDIAN brightness to land close to its average (a median
  near 85-110 and a mean near 95-115 is the target) — when the median sits far below the mean,
  the picture is one spotlight in a dark room, which is wrong here.
- The floor must never fall below about #6b4a38 (luminance about 75/255) anywhere in the image,
  including the corner furthest from the lamp. Only gentle falloff. Cool blue moonlight fill
  (#8fb0d8) keeps the far corners visible.

RULES
- Top-down only. No horizon line, no perspective, no vertical surfaces, no ceiling.
- The open floor must stay open: check that at least four fifths of the image is unobstructed

- Every material, floor and light source must run straight off the image edge. Nothing may stop
  at the edge, fade out at the edge, or be centred on the edge.
- No characters, no people, no creatures, no text, no letters, no numbers, no legible writing, no
